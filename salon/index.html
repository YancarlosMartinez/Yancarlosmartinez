For Trish

Patricia Michelle Candillo, “Ms. Trish” to so many

Trish spent her life caring for others. She had been a Certified Nursing Assistant since graduating from Hartford High School. She worked as a Patient Care Technician at Hartford Hospital, drove children and parents to their meetings for the Department of Children and Families, and even drove school buses for a while.

She was also a fashionista. She graduated from John Casablancas’ fashion modeling and makeup school as a teenager and loved looking and feeling her best. But she knew beauty goes deeper than appearance. When someone is suffering, even a small act of self-care can bring comfort, dignity and a renewed sense of themselves.

Then she lived it. During weeks and months in the hospital, she longed to get her hair and nails done but couldn’t leave. Even washing and styling her hair or taking a good shower was hard. Before she passed away, she imagined a salon inside the hospital for patients, parents and staff who need to freshen up without leaving the people they love.

Trish passed away on July 12, 2026, at the age of 33, after her battle with RMS, a rare cancer found mainly in children. She was the daughter of Patrick M. Candillo and Attorney Sylvia D. Reid, and she and her mom were incredibly close. Now her family and Gateway To Life, Inc. are building her vision.

“Gratitude is my attitude!”

Trish’s saying

Not just a salon. An oasis.

For patients who can’t leave because of ongoing treatment, for the parents sitting beside them through long stays, and for the staff who care for them. A place to sit down, relax and feel cared for while staying close to the people you love.

  • Haircuts
  • Hair washing and styling
  • Manicures and pedicures
  • Other personal care
  • Carefully vetted, qualified technicians
  • Medical permission and safety protocols

The hospital would own the space, with its own policies, licensing and insurance, so every service is provided safely.

“When you cannot control what is happening inside your body, being able to control something as simple as how you look and feel can become incredibly meaningful to recovery.”
